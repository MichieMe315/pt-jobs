BEFORE YOU ACCEPT AN OFFER

Compare the contract, not just the headline rate.

A fee split is revenue, not guaranteed take-home pay. Ask the clinic these questions before deciding.

01

Visits and cancellations

How many completed appointments are realistic? Are late cancellations or no-shows paid?

02

Benefits and paid time off

Does the employee offer include paid vacation, statutory holidays, health benefits or retirement contributions?

03

Expenses and administration

Who pays for professional insurance, licensing, supplies, charting time and other clinic costs?

04

Contract terms

Check notice periods, restrictive covenants, scheduling expectations and how compensation is calculated.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Salary and fee-split questions

Is a 60% split better than $50 per hour?

Not necessarily. Your contractor earnings depend on the clinic's fee per completed visit, the number of paid visits, your share of the fee and the weeks you work. Compare both offers above using your actual numbers.

What does “paid weeks per year” mean?

For an hourly employee, enter the number of weeks you receive wages, including paid vacation. Do not include weeks of unpaid leave. If your compensation is a fixed annual salary, select Annual salary instead.

What counts as a completed visit?

An appointment that is actually completed and eligible for payment under your clinic agreement. Do not assume cancelled appointments, no-shows or unpaid consultations generate contractor revenue.

Should I enter contractor expenses?

Yes, if you know them. Include annual professional and business costs you personally pay, such as liability insurance, licensing, bookkeeping, supplies and other eligible expenses. The calculator deducts them from contractor revenue before tax; it does not calculate tax deductions.

Does this include income tax, CPP, benefits or vacation?

No. This is a pre-tax comparison. Contractor earnings do not automatically include employee benefits or paid leave, and tax treatment depends on your circumstances. Use the results to ask better questions—not as a take-home-pay estimate.

How do I save or download my comparison?

Save your comparison to revisit it later. Sign in or create a job seeker account; new accounts require administrator approval.
